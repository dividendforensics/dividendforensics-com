# DFB filing brief publishing contract

## Scope
One original English Daily Brief newsletter per Asia/Seoul publication date, at most. The public format is a title, date, three or four short paragraphs (usually around 150–220 words), and primary-source links below. It is a website entry, not an email subscription or sending service. REITs, BDCs and operating companies (including large-cap companies) are eligible. Importance and reliable new primary evidence determine selection, not a dividend/growth binary. The scheduler is managed outside this repository; this code neither fetches news nor schedules or publishes remote changes.

No material new filing, no complete independent review, or failed build means **skip**. Keep the last published brief and its original date. Never manufacture weekend filler, backdate a story, refresh an old timestamp to appear current, or add empty daily pages. Existing Benzinga links and original attribution remain intact. The prose is original concise financial-news English, not copied Benzinga text.

## Data and review workflow
1. Keep all unapproved drafts OUTSIDE the public repository and upload manifest. Tests use entirely synthetic fixtures, never unpublished company research. Only independently approved content may enter the public catalog.
2. Open the actual SEC filing and issuer release/supplement. Capture direct URLs, source title, table/page locator, reporting start/end, event date, verified source publication date (or exact timestamp only when actually known), and SHA256 of the actual retrieved source bytes (not the URL/title). The primary event source must be an official SEC Archives filing URL on sec.gov or www.sec.gov. `event_source_id` identifies that source; `event_key` is `sec:` plus its 18-digit accession directory. The archive rejects repackaging that accession on another date or slug. Issuer supplements remain supporting sources, not SEC substitutes. When only a date is known, use published_date with publication_precision="date" and omit published_at. Do not invent midnight or equate SEC acceptance time with public availability. Optional accepted_at must have accepted_at_kind="SEC acceptance, not public availability" and an independently verified timezone. Source checked_at remains the actual capture/check timestamp. `event_date` is not the reporting-period end or build date. Reconcile company/ticker, non-GAAP definitions, units and share denominator.
3. Store input numbers as decimal **strings**, with source, units, period, basis and locator. `sum`, `subtract`, `ratio`, `ratio_percent` and `percent_change` recompute in Decimal. Record positive reported cash spending as reported and subtract explicitly. Calculations can reference earlier calculations, never forward references or cycles. Derived calculations use the full-precision intermediate rather than display rounding. Facts may use the main reporting period or an explicitly declared comparison period. Cross-period calculations require `comparison_basis`; rendered tables display each input period. Preserve signed adjustments. Enter independently expected results, not float output. Cross-check using a second method/source. Rounded per-share inputs warrant approximate narrative ratios, not implied precision. Unknown facts must be omitted or explicitly unresolved, never softened into apparently verified facts.
4. Write a short numerical hook and natural journalistic prose around two or three main points. Preserve definitions and decisive limitations. Keep fact/inference/open-question classification in the structured record, but do not display engineering labels, per-paragraph report headings, tables, raw variable names or formula lists. Use neutral informational wording. No buy/sell/hold instruction, target price, investment-safety guarantee, author-position implication, or invented no-position disclosure. No fabricated market reaction or price snapshot. Exact arithmetic is not proof of transcription, completeness or economic comparability.
5. A **different reviewer** opens the primary evidence and checks every numerical statement, noun, date, definition, grammar and word choice. Record six review results and meaningful evidence notes: sources, arithmetic, periods_units, english, neutrality and freshness. `freshness` must identify what materially changed, why this event matters now, and whether the subject duplicates a prior brief. No rubber-stamp generated approval text.
6. Set internal `published_at` to the actual editorial release-decision timestamp (NOT a claim of exact live-site availability); the public page displays only its KST publication date, `status` to `approved`, and compute `daily_briefs.digest(record)` over the final entire record. A review binds `brief_sha256` to that exact content. Review the final rendered page against the verified prose and evidence ledger: approval of an earlier Markdown draft does not approve a transformed JSON record or renderer output. Source-byte hashes aid traceability but do not establish source truth by themselves. `reviewed_at` follows `source_checked_at`; the exact-record final review may follow the editorial release decision, but must precede actual deployment. Neither record nor review timestamps may be future-dated. Changes to any content invalidate the review. Add the approved record and independent review to `content/daily-briefs/catalog.json` only after review. Do not alter existing publication dates or remove old records to make room.
7. Run the full site build and checks below; inspect generated detail/archive and mobile rendering. Commit/review the exact diff through the existing controlled PR workflow. Only the authorized root publisher merges after all required checks. Confirm public bytes and dates match the merged artifact; a local build is **not** deployment. The build rejects future editorial release-decision timestamps. Release only within that recorded KST calendar date. If CI/deployment crosses KST midnight, regenerate the release date and obtain a fresh hash-bound review before deployment. Confirm live availability separately; never backdate or present an editorial timestamp as the exact deployment instant.

The review hash is a change detector, **not a signature or proof of reviewer identity**. Access control and actual independent editorial review remain mandatory. Tests cannot prove sources true or guarantee perfect English. Never self-certify by inserting fabricated reviewer names/evidence to satisfy the gate.

## Native integration
- `daily_briefs.py` validates catalog, calculates results and renders with the existing `education.shell` / DFB CSS.
- `build_site.py` adds approved detail pages and `daily-briefs.html` to the existing sitemap, and a conditional section to the Home and Research landing pages. Existing external archive records are untouched.
- Empty catalog produces no new public page, link or sitemap record. A nonempty catalog renders a stable dated archive; each KST date admits only one brief. Same inputs produce identical outputs. Removing already published records fails closed rather than leaving orphaned pages.
- The public page shows verified narrative paragraphs and source links, not a duplicated machine ledger. Exact inputs, periods, source locators, units and calculations remain in the structured JSON for automated and independent review; they are not injected as hidden HTML. Every published number must still reconcile with that ledger, and essential definitions and limitations must remain in the prose. Sources are direct HTTPS links. Browser runtime makes no new third-party requests for this feature.
- `category` is an editorial taxonomy: `REITs`, `BDCs`, or `Operating Companies` (not an investment classification or recommendation).

## Revisions to already published entries
A prose/layout correction does not create another daily entry or reset its original publication date. Keep the source inputs and original publication timestamp unchanged unless correcting an actual error; obtain a fresh content hash and independent review when prose changes. Review the newly generated HTML digest too. The same-day first-release deadline does not require relabeling a previously published article as new.

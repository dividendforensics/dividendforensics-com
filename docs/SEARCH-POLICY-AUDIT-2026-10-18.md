## Findings that matter

The useful next step was to improve readers' ability to follow terms, attribution and dated evidence. Adding a nominal page or word target would not resolve those issues. Google describes clear sources, accurate authorship and financial-topic accuracy as aspects of helpful content, identifies trust as central to E-E-A-T, and says E-E-A-T is not itself one specific ranking factor. Its guidance expressly rejects the idea of a preferred word count. These are Search quality considerations, not a separate published AdSense YMYL approval formula. [Google Search: helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) (page updated 2026-10-05).

AdSense participation does invoke actual publisher rules: misleading representation, copied material without added value, low-value inventory, intrusive ad interaction and privacy disclosures are relevant. Passing local metadata tests does not settle those judgments. The policy also incorporates Search spam rules, so the two systems overlap without being interchangeable. [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938?hl=en).

Large-scale pages created primarily to manipulate rankings can be spam regardless of how they were produced. The existing focus on US REITs, BDCs, explicit numerical assumptions and functional research tools supplies a better editorial basis than expanding unrelated topics. This is an editorial inference from the site's content and the policy, not a finding about Google's assessment of DFB. [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

### Hosting decision remains separate from Google review

The current [GitHub Pages limits](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits) exclude using Pages as free hosting to run an online business or e-commerce site, or a site primarily facilitating commercial transactions or providing commercial SaaS. They also impose a 1 GB published-site limit, a soft 100 GB monthly bandwidth limit and a 10-minute deployment timeout; the soft ten-builds-per-hour limit has an exception for custom GitHub Actions publishing. Repository size or a successful build cannot establish that a business use is acceptable.

Read that alongside [GitHub Acceptable Use Policies, advertising section](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies#10-advertising-on-github): advertising is not generally prohibited, and its primary-focus restriction explicitly includes Pages. The reviewed documentation does **not** expressly approve or prohibit the particular case of an informational finance publication supported by AdSense. It would be inaccurate to infer either a blanket ban on every ad or unconditional permission for DFB's planned business from these texts.

**Remaining operator decision:** choose hosting for the actual monetized publication and any future paid offering, considering both restrictions. Continuing with Pages requires an operator judgment that the intended use fits those terms; choosing a host that expressly supports that commercial model is another route. This is an unresolved hosting suitability question, not a finding that the existing educational site is illegal or that Google has rejected it. No migration, DNS change, account action or external support inquiry was made. This audit therefore does not claim every non-code AdSense launch dependency has been eliminated.

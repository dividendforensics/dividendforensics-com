Operating companies: follow the cash

Follow earnings into operating cash, define your capital-spending deduction, and reconcile dividends and buybacks with the full cash statement.

Stock basics

New to the vocabulary? Start with how stocks are classified and tickers, exchanges, indexes, and ETFs. These introductions are separate from the REIT and BDC lesson sequences.

A new starting point beyond REITs and BDCs

One year. Two bridges. Four questions.

New to company accounts? Start with how the three financial statements connect, then return to the detailed cash-flow route below.

A manufacturer, software business or consumer company can report rising earnings while cash generation weakens. It can also distribute more cash than a particular free-cash-flow definition produced during the year. Neither observation explains the cause on its own. Start by rebuilding the statement, then separate the accounting reconciliation from your interpretation of capital allocation.

01 / EARNINGSWhat was earned?

Start with the net-income line used in the operating cash reconciliation.

02 / OPERATIONSWhat became cash?

Preserve the signs of adjustments and operating assets and liabilities.

03 / ALLOCATIONWhat was spent?

Deduct defined PP&E purchases, then actual dividends and repurchases.

04 / BALANCEWhere did cash go?

Reconcile all reported categories to the ending cash balance.

The earnings-to-cash bridge helps explain the source of operating cash flow. The beginning-to-ending cash bridge captures investing, financing and currency effects as well. A residual from the first bridge is not the change in the bank balance.

Collect the evidence before entering numbers.

Use one annual column and the same consolidation perimeter throughout.
Source locationBring into the worksheetQuestion to retain
Statement of cash flowsNet income; every operating adjustment; reported OCFDo all entered components sum to the published total?
Investing sectionCash PP&E purchases and the full signed investing totalWhat acquisitions, asset sales or securities movements sit outside PP&E purchases?
Financing sectionPaid dividends, paid share repurchases, and the full financing totalAre announcements, unsettled purchases or noncash transactions being confused with cash paid?
Cash reconciliation and notesBeginning and ending cash under the identical definition; currency effectsDoes the statement include restricted cash that the balance-sheet headline excludes?

Preserve source labels next to your grouped inputs. An issuer’s “other” adjustment is not automatically noncash; a signed subtotal must still be traceable to the individual source rows. A zero requires evidence. A missing number stays missing.

Build an explanation, not a score.

After the arithmetic reconciles, compare several complete annual periods and read the liquidity discussion. Investigate whether cash improved through stronger trading, delayed supplier payments, reduced inventory, disposals or new borrowing. A single year cannot distinguish a durable improvement from timing. Research reinvestment commitments and debt maturities separately; the workbench does not estimate them.

For property and lending businesses, use the REIT curriculum or BDC curriculum. For a source record across sectors, use the research worksheet. The filing labs connect the lessons to historical disclosures.

Sources, scope and next steps

This material covers annual statements for US nonfinancial operating companies, using US GAAP presentation and one reporting currency. The workbench fixes the unit at USD million. Banks, insurers, REITs and BDCs require different analytical frameworks; cross-border accounting classifications also need separate review. Examples are synthetic unless an identified historical filing is loaded.

  • SEC: Beginners’ Guide to Financial Statements — the cash-flow statement and its relationship to earnings.
  • SEC staff: Non-GAAP Financial Measures, Question 102.07 — explain the FCF definition and its limitations; other obligations may remain after capital expenditures.

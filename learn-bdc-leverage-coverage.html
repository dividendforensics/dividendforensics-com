BDC leverage and asset coverage: a descriptive ratio is not a compliance test

Connect debt, common equity and asset values while distinguishing a debt/equity worksheet from statutory asset coverage and borrowing covenants.

Before you start

BDC NAV and market discounts: separate the valuation from the trading price, Non-accruals at cost and fair value: read the exposure and the mark

  • Explain how asset marks change leverage without new borrowing.
  • Distinguish descriptive debt/equity from statutory asset coverage.
  • Identify the scope and conditions needed before assessing borrowing headroom.

Three different leverage questions require three definitions

Debt divided by common equity is a descriptive balance-sheet relationship. Statutory asset coverage is a legal calculation with its own treatment of assets, liabilities and senior securities. A credit agreement can impose another covenant definition. These measures are related but not interchangeable. Read which asset-coverage framework the particular BDC has adopted, the applicable conditions and the issuer's disclosed calculation. Investor.gov describes borrowing of up to two dollars for one dollar of equity under certain conditions; that statement is not permission to apply a universal two-times debt/equity rule to every instrument, entity and transaction. The SEC staff explanation describes lowering the requirement from 200% to 150% subject to approvals and other conditions; 150% is not automatic for every BDC.

Valuation losses consume equity before debt disappears

With debt principal unchanged, a decline in investment value generally reduces the residual equity and raises a simple debt/equity ratio. No new borrowing is necessary for leverage to rise. That mechanism can constrain flexibility at the same time credit conditions make asset sales or equity issuance more difficult. Conversely, a repayment funded with available cash reduces both assets and debt, and its ratio effect depends on the starting structure. Reconcile marks, liabilities and capital transactions before attributing an entire ratio change to management's financing decisions. A carrying-value movement in debt also needs its own explanation.

Exemptions, preferred claims and entity boundaries matter

The issuer may disclose excluded or specially treated borrowings, including applicable subsidiary arrangements, while preferred securities and other liabilities complicate the relationship between common equity and regulatory measures. These details are reasons to read the actual senior-securities table, borrowing notes and governing rules, not reasons to improvise a favorable exemption. A proportionate JV exposure table is another analytical view and need not match the accounting or regulatory perimeter. Keep it separate unless the relevant definition explicitly incorporates those exposures. The simplified calculator intentionally makes no statutory pass/fail judgment and does not estimate legally permitted additional borrowing.

Cash adjustment is a scenario, not a cancellation of obligations

Subtracting cash from debt can describe a chosen economic position, but it does not repay creditors or establish that the cash is unrestricted and available to the same entities. Keep gross debt visible beside any cash-adjusted figure. Funding commitments, settlement needs, facility restrictions and ordinary operating requirements can limit the amount available for debt reduction. A reported low ratio also does not reveal maturity concentration, security ranking or interest sensitivity. Pair a balance-sheet measure with a dated maturity and liquidity bridge, and identify which future marks or transactions would narrow the room rather than convert one ratio into a safety score.

Synthetic example / not company data

Synthetic: the asset mark changes both ratios

Assume only assets of 2,500, debt of 1,500 and common equity of 1,000, with no other claims. Debt/equity is 1.5× and the simplified assets/debt ratio is 166.6667%. A 250 asset markdown leaves assets of 2,250 and equity of 750, with debt unchanged. Debt/equity becomes 2.0× and simplified assets/debt becomes 150%. This is deliberately elementary balance-sheet arithmetic, not a statutory compliance calculation. Real asset coverage must apply the issuer's actual definitions, exclusions, senior claims and conditions before describing any threshold or remaining capacity.

Test your reasoning

If assets fall by 100 from the initial synthetic balance sheet while debt stays 1,500, what is simple debt/equity?

Reveal the worked answer

Equity becomes 900 and debt/equity is 1,500 / 900 = 1.6667×. That descriptive increase does not itself establish the issuer's statutory or covenant result.

A common mistake

Treating a simplified debt/equity result as statutory approval to borrow, or using a net-cash adjustment to erase gross obligations.

What to check in the disclosures

  • Read the issuer's adopted framework and conditions.
  • Distinguish principal, carrying value and covenant debt.
  • Keep common equity separate from preferred claims.
  • Show gross and cash-adjusted measures with dated scope.

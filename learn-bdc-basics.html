What is a BDC? Follow the fund, the borrowers and the shareholder

Understand the closed-end structure, the difference between borrower interest and shareholder distributions, and why a traded share is not a redeemable loan.

DFB Education · Beginner route · Updated 18 Oct 2026
In this guide

Before you start

No prior BDC lessons required.

  • Draw the legal claim from shareholder to BDC to portfolio company.
  • Separate a listed share's liquidity from the liquidity of private investments.
  • Identify the business questions that a quoted yield leaves unanswered.
01

Three parties, three different claims

A business development company is an investment company that has elected a particular regulatory framework; it is not simply another name for every private-credit fund. A useful first diagram has three boxes: the shareholder, the BDC and the businesses in its portfolio. A portfolio borrower owes obligations under its own loan documents. The BDC owns that investment, incurs its own expenses and financing costs, and may declare distributions to common shareholders. Buying a common share does not give its holder a direct contractual right to collect one borrower's interest or require a borrower to repay the shareholder personally. Its portfolio can include loans and equity in smaller or middle-market businesses; the vehicle's actual strategy must still be identified. BDC regulatory status and RIC tax treatment are separate concepts, explored later in the tax lesson.

02

Closed-end does not mean the portfolio is frozen

A closed-end structure does not ordinarily give every shareholder the open-ended redemption right familiar from a conventional mutual fund. A listed shareholder generally exits by selling to another market participant. Meanwhile, the BDC can originate investments, receive repayments, change financing and sometimes issue or repurchase shares. Those activities change the portfolio even if the vehicle remains closed-end. Non-traded BDCs require separate attention to their disclosed liquidity programs and limitations; a possible repurchase offer is not the same as exchange trading or a promise that every requested exit will be met. Non-traded alone does not identify the offering route or transfer restrictions: inspect the actual prospectus or private-offering documents rather than assuming all unlisted vehicles provide the same access or exit terms.

03

Read the income and the capital value together

A distribution is a transfer from the vehicle to its shareholders, not proof that every loan is performing or that the share price must remain unchanged. Ask what investments generated recognized income, how much was received in cash, what expenses sit ahead of common shareholders, and whether fair-value losses are eroding net assets. A BDC may report income while an investment's value deteriorates. Conversely, one low-income quarter may include timing or fee effects rather than a permanent loss of principal. Keep those possibilities separate instead of compressing the whole business into its latest annualized yield.

04

Make the first reading a map rather than a verdict

Before comparing companies, record whether the shares are listed, the investment strategy, the management arrangement and the reporting date. Identify debt, equity and other instruments in the portfolio rather than assuming every position is a first-lien loan. Locate the borrowing note and the distribution disclosures, then mark what remains unknown. Investor.gov's BDC bulletins are useful for the structure and risks; the issuer's own filing is needed for its actual assets and terms. Neither the label BDC nor filing with the SEC means the investment is insured, suitable for a particular person or approved as a good investment.

Synthetic example / not company data

Synthetic: the BDC is between the loan and the dividend

Assume a fictional BDC recognizes 12 of investment income during one period, incurs 3 of financing expense and 2 of operating expenses, and therefore has 7 before any other items in this simplified illustration. If common distributions are 6, the difference is 1. That 1 is not a complete cash-flow reconciliation: income may include noncash amounts and other cash movements may exist. A separate 4 fair-value decline can reduce net assets even though the simplified income-minus-distribution difference is positive. All amounts are invented and use the same monetary scale.

Test your reasoning

In the synthetic example, does the 1 income-minus-distribution difference prove that net assets grew?

Reveal the worked answer

No. The separate 4 valuation decline and other changes must enter the net-asset bridge. Income coverage, cash generation and capital preservation are different questions.

A common mistake

Treating a BDC share as if it were a direct, insured deposit or a loan redeemable at NAV.

What to check in the disclosures

  • Identify the actual listed or non-traded vehicle and share class.
  • Read the investment strategy and portfolio instruments.
  • Separate BDC borrowings from portfolio-company debt.
  • Record the declared distribution and reporting period.

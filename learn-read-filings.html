How to read a REIT filing without getting lost

A practical reading sequence for the 10-K, 10-Q, earnings release and supplemental operating tables.

DFB Education · Beginner learning path · 3 min read · Updated 18 Oct 2026 · Editorial approach
In this guide
  1. Start with a question and the right period
  2. Read the business before the ratios
  3. Tie the release back to the statements
  4. Keep a reproducible evidence trail
  5. Worked example
  6. Sources & next steps
01

Start with a question and the right period

A filing is easier to read when you have a question: did recurring income per share improve, are leases rolling at higher rents, or is a maturity becoming expensive? Start with the latest annual 10-K for the business and audited statements, then the subsequent 10-Qs for changes. Earnings releases and supplements can add property-level detail and non-GAAP reconciliations. Check publication dates and reporting periods. A quarter, a year-to-date amount and full-year guidance are not interchangeable.

02

Read the business before the ratios

The business section explains what the company owns and how it earns money. Risk factors identify exposures, although not every risk has equal probability. Management's discussion and analysis explains movements in results and liquidity. Read geographic, tenant and property concentrations alongside the operating tables. A tenant's percentage of annualized rent is not necessarily its percentage of cash collected, and a headline occupancy rate may exclude properties under development.

03

Tie the release back to the statements

Follow the reconciliation from net income to FFO and any adjusted metric. Mark each adjustment as recurring, non-recurring, cash or non-cash where the evidence supports that classification. Then read the cash-flow statement and notes on debt, leases, acquisitions and joint ventures. A favorable adjusted number can coexist with substantial financing needs. Record both the issuer's explanation and any unresolved reconciliation differences instead of filling gaps with assumptions.

04

Keep a reproducible evidence trail

Save the document title, filing date, page or table, units and the exact measure used. Note whether figures are consolidated or proportionate, common-share or total-company, and basic or diluted per share. A sensible counterargument to a pessimistic balance-sheet view may be available liquidity or contractual rent growth. A counterargument to optimistic earnings guidance may be capex excluded from the measure. The objective is to make both readings testable from documents, not to select only supportive facts.

Work through the numbers

Hypothetical: annualizing a quarter

An earnings release reports $0.80 of quarterly FFO per diluted share. Multiplying by four gives a $3.20 annualized illustration, not full-year guidance. A disposition in the next quarter or seasonal hotel results can make that shortcut misleading. Label the period, and use actual trailing figures or management guidance separately.

A common mistake

An earnings presentation is a useful map, but its selected charts are not a substitute for the financial statements and notes.

What to check in the disclosures

  • Record the document date, period, units and table location.
  • Read non-GAAP definitions and reconciliations.
  • Check debt and joint-venture notes.
  • Write down what remains unknown and the next disclosure that could resolve it.

Sources & scope

This guide focuses on US REITs. Examples are hypothetical unless explicitly attributed. Definitions and issuer disclosures can change; use the current source and the issuer's own reconciliation.

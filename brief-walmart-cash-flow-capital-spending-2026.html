Walmart’s Operating Cash Rises, but Capital Spending Pulls Free Cash Flow Lower

By DFB editorial20 November 2026 · KST

Walmart (NASDAQ: WMT) generated more operating cash in the nine months ended October 31, 2026, yet its free cash flow fell. The company’s November 19 release reported $29.436 billion of operating cash and $22.621 billion of payments for property and equipment, leaving $6.815 billion under its free-cash-flow definition.

A year earlier, operating cash was $27.452 billion and property-and-equipment payments were $18.627 billion, producing $8.825 billion of free cash flow. The arithmetic explains the reversal: operating cash increased by $1.984 billion, but capital spending rose by $3.994 billion. Free cash flow therefore declined by $2.010 billion.

Walmart attributed the capital-spending increase to its omnichannel growth strategy. Operating cash also reflected payment timing and inventory receipts, so its improvement should not be read solely as a change in underlying profitability. The cash-flow statement separately shows $5.913 billion of dividends and $7.830 billion of company-stock purchases.

These are nine-month totals, not third-quarter cash flows alone. Walmart’s non-GAAP free cash flow is not residual cash available for discretionary spending: its definition does not deduct debt service, other contractual obligations or acquisition payments. Whether operating cash can keep pace with investment remains a question for subsequent periods, not a conclusion from this release.

Sources: Walmart Q3 FY2027 earnings release, SEC Exhibit 99.1 · Walmart Q3 FY2027 earnings release.

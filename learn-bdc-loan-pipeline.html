From origination to repayment: how a BDC investment earns and loses money

Trace a loan through commitment, funding, recognized income, amendments, valuation and exit without confusing commitments with invested assets.

Before you start

What is a BDC? Follow the fund, the borrowers and the shareholder

  • Distinguish commitments, funded principal and fair value.
  • Separate borrower interest from principal repayment.
  • Map an investment's contractual seniority and exit risks.
01

A commitment is not the same as money already invested

Start with the investment's life cycle. A BDC may agree to provide financing before the borrower draws the full amount. A headline commitment therefore need not equal the funded principal or the carrying value shown in the schedule of investments. Undrawn commitments can create future funding needs at the BDC even when no interest-producing principal is yet outstanding. Follow the legal entity and instrument, the funding dates, the amount drawn and the conditions for additional draws. Do not add committed capacity and funded investments together as if both were separate earning assets.

02

Identify which payment changes income and which changes principal

Cash interest compensates the lender for providing capital, while principal repayment returns invested capital. Payment-in-kind interest can increase a contractual balance instead of arriving as current cash, subject to the issuer's recognition policy. Origination discounts, fees and prepayment charges may enter accounting income on different schedules. A loan's stated coupon is consequently only one part of the earnings story. Before comparing yields, establish whether the reported measure includes fee accretion, equity distributions, repayment fees or other items, and whether its denominator is amortized cost, fair value, average invested assets or another base.

03

Seniority describes a claim, not a guaranteed recovery

The words first lien, second lien, subordinated and equity help locate contractual claims, but they do not state an eventual recovery amount. Collateral quality, additional permitted debt, intercreditor terms, restructuring costs and the operating business all matter. The borrower may also have obligations elsewhere in its group. A sponsor's involvement does not automatically create a sponsor guarantee. Read maturity, cash-pay and PIK terms, covenants, collateral and any guarantee independently. If an investment is amended, ask what economic concession was made and what consideration the lender received rather than treating every extension as either a cure or a default. A unitranche label does not eliminate priority differences created by first-out and last-out arrangements; inspect the actual allocation and enforcement terms rather than deriving recovery from a marketing category.

04

Connect exit events to the next investment opportunity

A repayment can release cash and generate a fee, but it can also remove a high-spread asset. The BDC must then retain cash, repay its own debt, fund commitments or reinvest under the conditions available at that time. A sale below amortized cost produces a realized loss, while the current-period accounting result also depends on marks already recorded. Follow the investment's prior carrying value before describing a new loss. The source-reading task is to connect the investment schedule, income-recognition policy, cash-flow statement and subsequent events, preserving uncertainty when the filing does not disclose the precise cause of a change.

Synthetic example / not company data

Synthetic: cash received is not all income

A fictional loan has funded principal of 100, with a separate undrawn commitment of 20. During the period it pays 2 of cash interest, adds 1 of contractual PIK and repays 10 of principal. Ignoring discounts, fees and other changes, ending principal is 100 + 1 − 10 = 91. Cash received is 12, but only the 2 interest payment is cash interest; the 10 is returned principal. The 20 undrawn commitment is a separate future obligation. A fair value of 85 would be another measurement, not an additional loan to add to the 91.

Test your reasoning

If the borrower draws another 5 after the example's closing date, is the original 12 of cash received evidence that the BDC can meet that draw?

Reveal the worked answer

Not by itself. The 12 includes returned principal and may already have been reinvested or used elsewhere. Establish the later liquidity position, other commitments and legally available funding.

A common mistake

Counting principal repayments as recurring income, or adding an undrawn commitment to the funded asset balance.

What to check in the disclosures

  • Reconcile opening principal, PIK, draws, repayments and ending principal.
  • Keep amortized cost and fair value in separate columns.
  • Read collateral, ranking, covenants and guarantees independently.
  • Track undrawn commitments and the cash needed to fund them.

I.
Fortnight of XXVI · V · MMXXVI
On Method

The Buffer Half-Life™, and why time is the second axis.

Most yield analysis flattens time. A coverage ratio is a snapshot. A spread is a snapshot. A rating is a snapshot. Each one is true in the instant. None of them describe how long the truth will hold.

The Buffer Half-Life™ is the Bureau's way of restoring time as the second axis. Where the snapshot answers "is the dividend covered today?", the half-life answers "how long does the cushion last before something must change?". Same data, different question.

A buffer is not a number. It is a number divided by a clock.

This is why the Bureau labels engines rather than yields. Two 9% headlines can have the same first number and very different second numbers. The Cabinet rooms exist to do the second number.

— On Method · J.M.G.

II.
Fortnight of XXVI · V · MMXXVI
On Language

Three Clocks™ — the grammar of refinancing.

Three Clocks™ is the Bureau's way of separating the three time horizons that the word "credit" silently bundles together. Coverage today is the first clock — operating cash flow against committed distribution. Maturity tomorrow is the second — the schedule of debt coming due. Market access when tomorrow arrives is the third — the price at which the issuer can roll.

Three clocks, not one. Each one can be reading green while another reads red.

A name can be solvent on the first clock, exposed on the second, and orphaned on the third — in the same quarter.

The grammar matters because the language of yield analysis tends to collapse the three back into one. "Sustainable" hides which clock the speaker is reading. The Bureau's habit is to name the clock before naming the verdict.

— On Language · J.M.G.

III.
Fortnight of XXVI · V · MMXXVI
On Position

The BBB- Cliff™, and what a threshold is for.

The BBB- threshold is not a number on a ratings agency's page. It is a boundary in the architecture of who-can-hold-what. Above it: most insurance mandates, most pension fund frameworks, most large-cap bond index inclusions. Below it: a different ecosystem.

The BBB- Cliff™ is the Bureau's name for what happens at that boundary — not in the issuer's spreadsheet but in the structural composition of who buys the next refinancing. When an issuer crosses down, the buyer pool changes. When an issuer crosses up, the same is true in reverse, more slowly.

A rating is a price. The cliff is what changes when the price changes by one notch.

This is why the Bureau watches the migration, not the absolute level. A name has been investment-grade for ten quarters is one signal. A name moved from investment-grade to crossover in the last two is a different signal. The threshold is the same. The trajectory is what does the work.

— On Position · J.M.G.

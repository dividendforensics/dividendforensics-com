— Chamber Current —

On three calendars, and what the curve does not publish.

Rotation V · MMXXVI · Filed XVII · V · MMXXVI · Reading time ≈ 14 min

The dividend moves on a calendar. The refinancing clock moves on a curve. Calendars are published quarterly, by the issuer, well in advance. Curves are not — they are inferred from spread, from secondary market depth, from the conditional behaviour of the marginal lender on the day refinancing is required.

That asymmetry is the source of most misunderstanding about dividend durability. The reader sees the calendar; the buffer is paying for the curve. When the calendar continues and the curve has moved, the appearance of continuity is preserved while the structure has changed.

"The dividend moves on a calendar. The refinancing clock moves on a curve. Calendars are published. Curves are not."

This chamber walks through three illustrative cases — an investment-grade equity REIT with a 2027 maturity wall, a BDC managing non-accrual drift, and a covered-call ETF whose premium share is being thinned by volatility compression. In each case the calendar reads continuous; the curve has moved underneath.

The Bureau frames the difference using the Three Clocks™ lens: Coverage today, Maturity tomorrow, Market Access when tomorrow arrives. The point of the lens is not prediction; it is to clarify which clock the dividend is paying from, and which clock is moving against the issuer in silence.

— Chamber Past · Rotation IV —

The four sources of yield, read separately.

Rotation IV · Filed III · V · MMXXVI · Reading time ≈ 9 min

What the screen labels as "yield" is structurally four different promises: AFFO from an equity REIT, NII from a BDC, option premium from a covered-call ETF, and return of capital from any vehicle distributing more than it earns. Each promise has a different durability profile.

A 7% headline yield from an investment-grade REIT, paid out of AFFO with a 71% payout ratio, is one structure. The same 7% headline from a high-yield BDC, paid out of NII that depends on the credit performance of underlying middle-market borrowers, is another. From a cov-call ETF where two-thirds of the distribution is option premium, it is a third structure. From a CLO equity vehicle paying ROC, it is a fourth.

The Bureau separates them. The screen does not.

— Chamber Past · Rotation III —

A short note on humility.

Rotation III · Filed XX · IV · MMXXVI · Reading time ≈ 5 min

The Bureau publishes diagnostics, not advice. Diagnostics can be wrong. When they are wrong, the correction is filed in the Errata Desk; the original is not deleted; the strikethrough remains visible alongside the corrected version.

This is not a stylistic choice. A research institution that erases its own past readings is no longer a research institution. The continuity of the record — including the parts that were wrong — is what makes the rest of the record readable.

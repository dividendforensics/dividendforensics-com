Apollo Closes $600 Million Debt Sale With a 2036 Rate Reset

By DFB editorial15 November 2026 · KST

Apollo Global Management (NYSE: APO) completed a $600 million sale of junior subordinated notes on November 13, according to a new SEC filing. The transaction moves a previously announced financing from pricing to issuance, adding debt intended for general corporate purposes, including its planned investment in Mubadala Capital Asset Management.

The notes mature on December 15, 2056, but the initial 7.550% annual interest rate is fixed only until December 15, 2036. After that first reset date, the rate for each reset period becomes the applicable five-year U.S. Treasury rate plus 2.672 percentage points. Apollo also has the right to defer interest payments.

The headline borrowing amount is not the cash available to spend. In its November 9 pricing announcement, Apollo estimated proceeds of approximately $594 million after the underwriting discount but before offering expenses. Those additional expenses still reduce the proceeds; the estimate should not be treated as a final cash balance.

The closing confirms that the notes were issued, not that the proceeds have already been invested. The filing does not establish the return on the planned investment, and today’s coupon does not determine the borrowing cost after the reset.

Sources: Apollo Form 8-K, November 13, 2026 · Apollo pricing announcement, November 9, 2026.

Internal and external BDC management: follow the expense contract

Compare management arrangements by what shareholders actually bear, including asset-based fees, incentive compensation, reimbursements and waivers.

Before you start

What is a BDC? Follow the fund, the borrowers and the shareholder, From origination to repayment: how a BDC investment earns and loses money

  • Separate management structure from investment performance.
  • Identify the fee base, expenses and incentive arrangements.
  • Compare net shareholder economics on a consistent scale.
01

Begin with who employs and pays the investment team

An internally managed BDC generally incurs the costs of its own investment professionals and operations. An externally managed BDC contracts with an investment adviser, often with separate administrative arrangements. These structures organize expenses differently; neither label alone demonstrates better underwriting, lower total shareholder costs or a more durable distribution. An internal team can have material compensation costs, and an external arrangement can include negotiated terms or waivers. Read the actual arrangement for the period under review instead of ranking two vehicles solely by whether their advisers sit inside or outside the corporate structure.

Synthetic example / not company data

Synthetic: the same fee rate can produce a different equity burden

Assume a fictional externally managed vehicle has 1,000 of common equity and 800 of debt funding 1,800 of assets. A deliberately simplified 1.5% annual fee on all assets is 27, equal to 2.7% of equity. Without the 800 of borrowing, the same fee rule would charge 15 on 1,000 of assets. This is not a complete expense ratio and not any issuer's actual contract: exclusions, tiers, incentive fees, interest and administration are omitted. An internally managed comparison must include its actual employee and operating costs rather than inserting zero.

02

A percentage means little until the base is identified

A management fee can be stated as a percentage of a defined asset base rather than of common equity. Borrowing can expand assets without increasing shareholder equity by the same amount, so the resulting charge relative to common net assets can differ from the headline percentage. Cash exclusions, tiers and averaging dates can also matter. Separately identify incentive fees, administration reimbursements and other operating expenses. A prospectus expense table and the accounting expense in a historical quarter answer related but different questions; do not mechanically add a projected table percentage to fees already deducted from reported NII.

03

Waivers and related businesses need a second column

A waived fee can improve current income while leaving a different expense burden after the waiver expires. Record whether the waiver is voluntary, contractual, temporary or subject to recoupment, using the governing documents. Some BDCs also earn income from managing other vehicles, which is a distinct business from paying an adviser to manage the BDC itself. Map the direction of each payment. An issuer explanation can help locate its structure, but statements about superior alignment or lower costs remain the issuer's perspective until supported by comparable definitions and evidence.

04

Compare the result after expenses without losing the explanation

Create a table of investment income, financing expense, base fees or internal operating cost, incentive expense and remaining NII on the same period and monetary basis. Then ask what changed when assets, leverage or compensation changed. A low expense ratio may reflect a large denominator, deferred spending or a waiver rather than permanently superior economics. A high ratio may include an unusual item that deserves a separately labeled comparison. Keep both the reported result and any analytical adjustment visible. The goal is to explain the source of differences, not manufacture one universally correct cost ranking.

Test your reasoning

In the example, does the increase from 15 to 27 prove that borrowing harmed shareholders?

Reveal the worked answer

No. It identifies an additional expense channel. Compare the income and risks from the additional assets with financing and all other costs before assessing the combined economics.

A common mistake

Comparing a fee expressed on gross assets with a cost expressed on common equity and calling the percentages directly comparable.

What to check in the disclosures

  • Locate the current advisory and administrative agreements.
  • Record the base, averaging dates, tiers and exclusions.
  • Separate waived, accrued and paid fees.
  • Include internal compensation and related-party reimbursements.

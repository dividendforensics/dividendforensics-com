Apple’s New Debt: $3.25 Billion Falls Due by 2029

By DFB editorial10 November 2026 · KST

Apple completed a $7.5 billion debt issue on November 9, selling seven series of senior unsecured notes. Of that principal, $3.25 billion matures in 2028 and 2029, about 43% of the new borrowing. The remaining maturities extend through 2036, according to its new SEC filing.

The issue combines $6.25 billion of fixed-rate notes, with annual coupons ranging from 4.65% to 5.35%, and $1.25 billion of floating-rate notes. The floating rates reset quarterly using compounded SOFR plus contractual spreads. These are the terms of this issue, not Apple’s entire debt portfolio.

The structure separates interest-rate exposure from repayment timing. Fixed coupons keep the stated interest rate unchanged, but the principal still comes due on schedule. Floating coupons leave interest payments exposed to changes in the reference rate. Neither feature establishes how Apple will fund the eventual repayments.

Apple’s prospectus estimated approximately $7.47 billion of net proceeds after underwriting discounts and expenses. It lists buybacks and dividends among several possible corporate uses, alongside investment and debt repayment. How much ultimately goes to each purpose remains unspecified; the borrowing alone does not demonstrate that dividends require debt funding.

Sources: Apple completion filing (Form 8-K) · Apple final prospectus supplement.

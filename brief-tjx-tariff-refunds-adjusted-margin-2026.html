TJX’s Tariff Refunds Change the Direction of Its Margin Comparison

By DFB editorial19 November 2026 · KST

TJX’s third-quarter pretax profit margin rose to 13.0% from 12.7% a year earlier, but the comparison changed after tariff refunds and related compensation expenses were excluded. The company reported an adjusted margin of 12.5%, below the prior-year level, in its November 18 earnings release.

For the quarter ended October 31, 2026, TJX received $132 million in refunds of previously paid International Emergency Economic Powers Act tariffs. It also accrued $55 million of incremental expenses for year-end incentive compensation and non-executive discretionary bonuses. Subtracting those expenses produces the $77 million net pretax benefit reported by the company.

That distinction matters when reading the margin trend. The reported measure improved by 0.3 percentage points, while the company’s adjusted measure declined by 0.2 points. The adjustment removes both the refund benefit and the related compensation expense accruals, rather than treating every dollar received as an addition to pretax profit.

The refund receipts and compensation accruals also describe different accounting events: an expense accrual does not establish when cash will be paid. Whether subsequent quarters show a similar margin pattern remains open. This quarter’s reconciliation does not establish recurring earnings or cash available for distributions.

Sources: TJX Q3 FY2027 earnings release, SEC Exhibit 99.1 · TJX third-quarter fiscal 2027 earnings release.

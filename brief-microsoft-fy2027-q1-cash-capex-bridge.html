Microsoft’s Cash-Capex Free Cash Flow Fell About 27% in Its Latest Quarter

Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) presents two questions in its latest cash-flow statement: how much cash remained after property-and-equipment spending, and how cash dividends compared with that measure.

Publication date 2026-10-31 KST · Event 2026-10-28 · Reporting period 2026-07-01 to 2026-09-30

Sources checked 2026-10-31T13:38:55.719030+00:00

fact

The Print

For the quarter ended September 30, Microsoft reported $58.0 billion of operating cash flow and $39.37 billion of cash additions to property and equipment. Subtracting the second from the first produces about $18.63 billion under DFB’s cash-capex free-cash-flow definition. The comparable calculation a year earlier was about $25.66 billion, implying a decline of about 27%. Calculations use the reported million-dollar figures before rounding for presentation; derived percentages should not imply greater measurement precision. SEC Form 10-Q, cash-flow statement, p. 6

fact

A Separate Dividend Calculation

Cash dividends paid were about $6.76 billion. Against the calculated free cash flow, that is about 36%; the defined cash-flow measure was approximately 2.8 times cash dividends paid. Subtracting those dividends leaves about $11.88 billion. That arithmetic remainder does not measure cash available for discretionary use; it excludes other investing and financing cash flows. The underlying cash-flow figures also appear in Microsoft’s earnings release. Microsoft FY2027 Q1 earnings release

inference

What the Comparison Does Not Establish

A falling cash-flow measure does not by itself establish that distributions exceed cash generation. Nor does positive coverage establish that future dividends are secure. Both conclusions would require more than one quarter and a consistent definition of the competing cash requirements.

fact

Lease Principal Remains Outside This Measure

Cash-capex free cash flow does not capture every infrastructure commitment. Microsoft’s lease note separately reports $1.0 billion of financing cash outflows from finance leases. Deducting that amount produces an additional analytical measure of about $17.63 billion. It is not the company’s reported free-cash-flow figure, and lease interest already reflected in operating cash flow should not be deducted again. SEC Form 10-Q, Note 12, p. 22

open question

The Next Comparison

The next-quarter comparison should use the same bridge: operating cash, cash capital spending, lease principal and distributions. Keeping those components separate makes changes visible without converting a quarterly coverage calculation into a forecast or an investment recommendation.

Limits

Cash-capex free cash flow is DFB-defined operating cash flow less cash additions to property and equipment, not an issuer-reported FCF figure. The additional lease-principal deduction is analytical. Derived figures are arithmetic on rounded reported inputs; the dividend remainder excludes other investing and financing cash flows. One quarter does not establish future distribution capacity.

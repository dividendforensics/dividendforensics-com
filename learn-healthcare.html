Healthcare REITs: properties, operators and reimbursement

Separate healthcare property ownership from operating exposure and examine the different risks in senior housing, hospitals and medical offices.

DFB Education · Intermediate learning path · 3 min read · Updated 18 Oct 2026 · Editorial approach
In this guide
  1. The label covers different cash flows
  2. Tenant health versus resident demand
  3. Competing views on operating leverage
  4. Disclosures to read before valuation
  5. Worked example
  6. Sources & next steps
01

The label covers different cash flows

Healthcare REITs can own medical offices, hospitals, skilled nursing facilities and senior housing. The building type does not fully explain the investment: the operating arrangement matters too. A landlord collecting contractual rent faces a different cash-flow profile from a structure whose results reflect operating revenue and expenses at the facility. Some portfolios contain both. Read the segment descriptions and ownership structure before comparing margins or occupancy, because the same sector label can conceal very different economic exposure.

02

Tenant health versus resident demand

Demographic demand is only a starting point. Operators must staff facilities, maintain service quality and collect payment from residents, insurers or government programs, depending on the business. An aging population does not guarantee that a particular operator can afford its lease. Where disclosed, rent coverage measures compare operator earnings with rent; definitions, reporting lags and exclusions matter. Coverage based on management estimates or an incomplete tenant sample deserves less confidence than directly comparable, comprehensive information.

03

Competing views on operating leverage

The positive case for senior housing may emphasize occupancy recovery spreading fixed costs over more residents. The cautious case emphasizes wages, staffing availability, liability exposure and residents' ability to pay. Medical offices can have a different demand and lease profile from hospitals or skilled nursing. Reimbursement sensitivity also differs by property and operator. A useful analysis builds separate scenarios by segment instead of assuming that all healthcare real estate is either defensive or strongly tied to the same demographic trend.

04

Disclosures to read before valuation

Review the segment split, operator concentration, occupancy definitions, same-store portfolio changes, capital commitments and any transition of troubled assets. For leased properties, inspect guarantees, rent deferrals and coverage disclosure. For operating exposure, look at revenue per occupied unit and controllable expenses alongside occupancy. AFFO is issuer-defined, and restructurings or operator support can complicate its interpretation. Cross-check adjustments against the cash-flow statement and annual-report discussion rather than treating an adjusted earnings label as evidence of readily distributable cash.

Work through the numbers

Hypothetical worked example

Consider a hypothetical 100-unit facility with 85 occupied units, each generating $4,000 monthly. Annual revenue is 85 × $4,000 × 12 = $4.08 million. If operating costs total $3.4 million, the simplified operating surplus is $680,000. Adding five occupied units at the same revenue adds $240,000 annually; with $120,000 incremental costs, surplus rises to $800,000. This excludes rent, corporate costs, financing and capital work, and it does not establish operator rent coverage without those definitions.

A common mistake

Demographic growth and landlord dividend safety are different claims. The path between them runs through operator profitability, contracts, funding and actual cash collection.

What to check in the disclosures

  • Separate leased and operating structures.
  • Check operator concentration and coverage definitions.
  • Identify reimbursement exposure by segment.
  • Inspect staffing costs and recurring capital needs.

Sources & scope

This guide focuses on US REITs. Examples are hypothetical unless explicitly attributed. Definitions and issuer disclosures can change; use the current source and the issuer's own reconciliation.

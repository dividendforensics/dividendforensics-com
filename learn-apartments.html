Apartment REITs: rent growth is only half the story

Read apartment occupancy, concessions, resident turnover and supply alongside operating expenses and recurring property investment.

DFB Education · Intermediate learning path · 3 min read · Updated 18 Oct 2026 · Editorial approach
In this guide
  1. A local housing business
  2. From advertised rent to collected revenue
  3. Competing views on supply and affordability
  4. Expenses, capital and disclosure checks
  5. Worked example
  6. Sources & next steps
01

A local housing business

Apartment REITs own rental communities, while the wider residential category also includes other housing models. The relevant market is often a neighborhood or commuting area, not the national housing market. Employment, household formation, homeownership affordability and competing supply affect demand differently across locations. Shorter residential leases allow rents to adjust relatively frequently, but they also expose the landlord to weakening market rents sooner. State and local rules can change what adjustments are permitted and when they become effective.

02

From advertised rent to collected revenue

Asking rents are not the same as achieved rents. Free months, move-in credits, bad debt and vacancy reduce effective revenue. Renewal rent growth describes residents who stay, while new-lease growth reflects incoming residents; their blend depends on turnover. Physical occupancy counts occupied units, whereas economic measures can incorporate lost revenue. Always read the definition. A portfolio can report positive renewal increases while new residents receive concessions, so the headline increase may overstate the direction of marginal demand.

03

Competing views on supply and affordability

An optimistic argument links expensive homeownership to sustained rental demand. A cautious argument notes that renters still face income constraints and can share housing, move or negotiate. New construction can pressure nearby landlords even if long-term demand is healthy. Conversely, a temporary construction slowdown does not automatically create pricing power in every submarket. Compare delivered units, properties still under construction and the location of the REIT's communities, rather than applying one national supply narrative to the whole portfolio.

04

Expenses, capital and disclosure checks

Property taxes, insurance, payroll, utilities and repairs can rise independently of rent. Same-store NOI helps compare an established pool but excludes properties according to issuer-specific rules and does not deduct interest or all capital investment. Inspect the inclusion period, renovation exclusions and occupancy calculation in the supplemental report. Distinguish apartment-turnover costs from major replacements and development commitments. If management presents AFFO, assess its treatment of recurring maintenance; the measure is not standardized and cannot replace the cash-flow statement.

Work through the numbers

Hypothetical worked example

A hypothetical 100-unit community charges $2,000 monthly and is 95% occupied all year. Before concessions, revenue is 100 × $2,000 × 12 × 95% = $2.28 million. With $900,000 of property expenses, simplified NOI is $1.38 million. If revenue grows 3% but expenses grow 8%, NOI becomes $2.3484 million − $972,000 = $1.3764 million, slightly lower despite rent-related revenue growth. Debt service, capital replacements and tax are excluded.

A common mistake

An attractive rent-growth figure can conceal rising concessions or expenses. Compare equivalent properties and consistent periods before drawing a conclusion about operating momentum.

What to check in the disclosures

  • Separate new leases from renewals.
  • Read concessions and bad-debt disclosure.
  • Map competing supply to actual submarkets.
  • Inspect same-store definitions and capital spending.

Sources & scope

This guide focuses on US REITs. Examples are hypothetical unless explicitly attributed. Definitions and issuer disclosures can change; use the current source and the issuer's own reconciliation.
